# Whether knowingly but unintentionally taking life violates the first precept?

## Bibliography

<!-- 

Notes:

Q&A with Tan Ajaan Geoff at Wat Metta outside the old kitchen 2/1/26. He was asked by Alex, a visitor with a strong British accent, if knowingly flushing a toilet with a live insect (I don't call if its being alive was stated explicitly, though it was implied clearly) to discard the waste, without any intention to harm or kill the insect, is a violation of the first precept. Tan Ajaan's response: no.

The relevant question is less whether it violates the precept, precepts are sometimes limited in scope by nature, and exclude related actions which are seemingly unethical, but whether it is a grievous act to knowingly but unintentionally kill a living being. In ordinary cases of this with human beings, it seems most agree it very much is. E.g., one in a rush to get to work decides not to brake for the crossing pedestrian, lest they be late, so they mow them down knowingly, killing them, but without any intention to kill them.
