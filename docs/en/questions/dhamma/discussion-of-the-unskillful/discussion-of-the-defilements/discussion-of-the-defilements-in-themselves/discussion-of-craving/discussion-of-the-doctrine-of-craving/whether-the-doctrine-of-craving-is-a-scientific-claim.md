# Whether the doctrine of craving is a scientific claim?

## Bibliography

<!-- 

Notes:

The term "scientific" needs to be clarified. What is intended is something along the lines of "scientific" in the sense of contemporary western science. This question is prompted by a question that could perhaps be asked about the doctrine of craving, though equally many other doctrines: Is there any scientific support for the doctrine of craving? The question suggests that the doctrine should be verifiable, or even insinuates that it lacks scientific support. It should be clarified what precisely the question is aiming at. Is it an epistemic question? I.e., how is knowledge pertaining to the Dhamma to be gained, i.e., is it scientific? Prima facie, it seems both desired that the doctrine of craving be consonant with science, and at least supportable thereby, and yet also to clarify that it is not fundamentally a scientific claim, as other claims are in the public material empirical sciences. The latter is what has to be spelled out carefully. There are various domains which seem to fundamentally lie outside of the domain of the material sciences, though they might and often do overlap therewith, such as ethics, math, logic, metaphysics, and so on. Is the Dhamma such a science? Is this science such a science?
