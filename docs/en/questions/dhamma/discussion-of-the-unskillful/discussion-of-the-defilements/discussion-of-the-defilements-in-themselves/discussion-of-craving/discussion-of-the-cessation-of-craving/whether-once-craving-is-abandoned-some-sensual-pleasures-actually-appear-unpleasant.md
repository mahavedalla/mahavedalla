# Whether, once craving is abandoned, some sensual pleasures actually appear unpleasant?

## Bibliography

<!-- 

Notes:

This question is relevant to the defense of renunciation from charges of the denial of the flesh. Such charges rest on the assumption that the renunciate renounces genuine pleasures. Yet, radically, the renunciate may argue that not only are at least most of said sensual pleasures not, in fact, very pleasant, but that some are even positively unpleasant. This view seems to be explicitly adopted in MN 75:

>evameva kho, māgaṇḍiya, atītampi addhānaṃ kāmā dukkhasamphassā ceva mahābhitāpā ca mahāpariḷāhā ca, anāgatampi addhānaṃ kāmā dukkhasamphassā ceva mahābhitāpā ca mahāpariḷāhā ca, etarahipi paccuppannaṃ addhānaṃ kāmā dukkhasamphassā ceva mahābhitāpā ca mahāpariḷāhā ca. ime ca, māgaṇḍiya, sattā kāmesu avītarāgā kāmataṇhāhi khajjamānā kāmapariḷāhena pariḍayhamānā upahatindriyā dukkhasamphassesuyeva kāmesu sukhamiti viparītasaññaṃ paccalatthuṃ.

As far as I am aware, the Buddha never spells out this view in detail, or provide a clear list of which sensual pleasures are unpleasant in the way indicated. The aforementioned quote refers to sensuality as such without qualification. This is somewhat ambiguous, since sensuality seems to involve many elements, as discussed elsewhere: e.g., the sense objects themselves, the pleasure thereof, sense bases, contact, etc. Moreover, pains have to be taken to spell out this quote without falling into inconsistency: namely, because a basic commitment of the Canon does seem to be that there is a genuine pleasure in sensual pleasures, with or without the perversion of craving and other defilements. The book and number escape me now, but I recall a sutta where the Buddha explicitly denies that there is no gratification in sensual pleasures. 

Here it seems relevant to note the different forms of gratification involved in sensuality: (1) the gratification of craving, and (2) the raw pleasant feeling (vedanā) of sensual pleasures. The aforementioned quote parallels a previously given simile of a leper. Taking the simile seriously, leprosy is craving, the burning charcoal pit is sensual pleasure, and the pleasure the leper feels from scorching his limbs over said pit is the pleasure one feels from indulging one's craving with said sensual pleasures. This interpretation is somewhat shaky, particularly regarding these last two points. Granting it for the moment, in this case, given that the above quote directly parallels a statement highlighting that the charcoal pit (sensual pleasure) is painful, it would seem to be that, analogously, the quote is claiming that analogously sensual pleasures themselves, not just the craving or the relief therefrom, are painful. It is unclear how this could be taken at face value without falling into inconsistency, as mentioned above. Thus, it seems some interpretive work is needed. 

Is it more plausible to say that simply some sensual pleasures which, for a mind perverted by craving, appear pleasant, are actually unpleasant, and this is seen when craving therefore is removed? Is this consistent with the Canon? Perhaps "kāma" here is being used in a restrictive sense?
