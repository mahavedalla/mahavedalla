# Whether celibacy should not be practiced because unnatural?

## Bibliography

<!-- 

Notes:

Whether celibacy can rightly be considered natural is still a matter unsettled by me. There are various ways to interpret the objection underlying the question: (1) celibacy should not be practiced simply because it is unnatural; (2) celibacy should likely not be practiced because it is not natural, and this latter fact is a good indication, even if provisional, of what is good for a living being.

It would seem that (1) commits a straightforward naturalistic fallacy, and thus can be set aside. (2) is a more reasonable position. I am not a zoologist or anthropologist. The fact that some behavior is natural for members of a species, or livings beings, may or may not generally be regarded by the relevant experts as a good indication, some inductive evidence, of what is actually in some sense suitable to or good for them, or moral (this is questionable). This would have to be confirmed. Nonetheless, it also seems clear that there also many cases where this is not true. Regarding morality, this doesn't seem to require any comment. Regarding the beneficial, well-being, and so on, the limitations of my knowledge in this area prevent me from saying much more. I have, however, heard biologists and anthropologists make some comments to the effect that some natural behaviors or evolutionary adaptations are not beneficial to individual (which at face value would seem to be the default assumption); that nature or evolution, if they can be so objectified, "looks after" the species, the result of which is the survival and selection of the fittest; and, in this way, well-being and so on are secondary or incidental.

It nonetheless seems that any inductive evidence will have to be weighed against other evidence and what just seems to be actual fact. Thus, that one is in the habit of drinking a coffee most mornings is good inductive evidence to think they will drink one this morning, but is discounted by the fact that we have good reason to think that this person is in such a rush they lack the time, or that they have a colonoscopy today, and is ultimately rejected (for lack of a better term) owing to the fact that they actually chose not to drink one this morning.
