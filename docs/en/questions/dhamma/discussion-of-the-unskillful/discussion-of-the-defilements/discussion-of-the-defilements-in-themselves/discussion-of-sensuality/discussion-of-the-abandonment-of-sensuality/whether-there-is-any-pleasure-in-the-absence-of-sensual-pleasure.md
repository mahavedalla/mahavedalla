# Whether there is any pleasure in the absence of sensual pleasure?

## Bibliography

<!-- 

Notes:

A common rejoinder to the doctrine of craving is that it seems like the absence of craving, and the degree of renunciation it requires, would leave the practitioner devoid of any pleasure; and, so the thought goes, there is something wrong with or undesirable about that. 

1. Most importantly, it is the very absence of sensual craving which is pleasant, in the sense of health, well being, etc.

2. The Canon seems to offer a robust array of pleasures that aren't connected with sensual pleasure: most importantly jhana, but I'm inclined to include the whole path; for instance, developing virtue can be very pleasant; studying the discourses can be very pleasant.
