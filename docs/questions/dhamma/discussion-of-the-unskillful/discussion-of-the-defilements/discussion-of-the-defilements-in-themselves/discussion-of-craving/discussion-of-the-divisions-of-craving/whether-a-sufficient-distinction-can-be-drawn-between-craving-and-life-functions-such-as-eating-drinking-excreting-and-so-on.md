# Whether a sufficient distinction can be drawn between craving and life functions, such as eating, drinking, excreting, and so on?

Notes:

A serious objection to the doctrine of craving is that it might not be able to draw a clear line between mental and physical almost everyone, including the ardent renunciate, wants to say are not inherently problematic as far as the four noble truths are concerned, and craving. For instance, one might worry that if we say craving is the cause of suffering, there is the risk that physical hunger gets implicated in that same craving; if so, this could lead to further issues like making liberation seem out of reach. It seems the obvious response is to say plainly that taṇhā does not include physical hunger itself, though physical hunger can certainly be a basis for craving (e.g., craving particular foods, toppings, in specific locations, and other gastronomical fantasies). The question then may naturally be raised: what is the difference between craving in the context of the four noble truths and physical hunger, or physical thirst, or the impulse to relieve oneself. Perhaps here one could appeal to a mind/body distinction: craving mental, hunger physical. Perhaps a convenient heuristic, though one might worry that the distinction is not so clear cut. It seems physical hunger, on a practical level, can't be spoken of other than as a sentient experience, requiring consciousness and other mental faculties. Perhaps, though, the distinction could be defended. One might consider just taking the distinction to be brute, primitive: there is craving, and there are phenomena like physical hunger, and they're different simply because they're different; moreover, one is the cause of suffering, and one is not. Someone here might be somewhat dissatisfied with this explanation, understandably: does not the distinction ask for some kind of explanation? Then again, one might think that in any serious philosophical discussion, the line has to be drawn somewhere, or at least in many, and this is a perfectly reasonable move.
