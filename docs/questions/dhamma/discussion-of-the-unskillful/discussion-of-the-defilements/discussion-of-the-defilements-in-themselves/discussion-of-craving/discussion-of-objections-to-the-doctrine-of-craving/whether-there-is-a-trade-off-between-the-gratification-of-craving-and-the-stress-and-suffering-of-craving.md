# Whether there is a trade-off between the gratification of craving and the stress and suffering of craving?

## Bibliography

<!-- 

Notes:

This question concerns the pleasure involved in the gratification of craving itself, not the raw pleasant qualities of the objects of craving. The issue is whether it is rational to accept the stress and suffering of craving in exchange for the pleasure that accompanies its satisfaction.

To avoid confusion, for now it seems it is best to coin a new term to describe the pleasure of sensuality according to the renunciate position: "maladaptive remedial pleasure". "Remedial" because the pleasure of gratifying craving is the remediation of stress, dissatisfaction, etc., of that very craving which, on the renunciate view, like the pangs of hunger or thirst. "Maladaptive" because that very pleasure exacerbates the underlying condition the relief of which it is induced by. Just as, say, the pleasure of scratching the site of an allergic reaction to poison oak might actually worsen that very poison oak. Perhaps another label could the "deflationary view or approach" to the pleasure/gratification of craving (and perhaps to sensuality at large). For instance, the Buddha has a deflationary approach to the pleasure of sensuality, which is why appealing to that pleasure to defend it falls flat, for him (given as example, not endorsed).
