# Whether the renunciation of sensual pleasures is life-negating?

## Bibliography

<!-- 

Notes:

The objection is something along these lines: 

(1) craving appears instrumental to the production of higher goods; (2) much ordinary happiness seems to arise from the gratification of craving; and (3) the renunciation of sensual pleasures appears to involve the loss of genuinely valuable pleasures. Taken together, this suggests that the doctrine is life-negating.

(1) As argued elsewhere, either what are taken to be “higher goods” are not genuinely so, or craving is not in fact necessary for the production of those higher goods that remain.

(2) As argued elsewhere, the gratification of craving is a maladaptive remedial pleasure—and is therefore not a positive good in its own right; indeed, freedom from craving and the need for its relief is itself a great good and pleasure.

(3) As argued elsewhere, the renunciation of sensual pleasures does not constitute a genuine loss to the renunciate. This is because (i) there are pleasures superior to sensual pleasure accessible contigent on the renunciation of sensual pleasures; (ii) the renunciate will want to maintain that sensual pleasures are in fact not actually great pleasures in the first place, or goods either; (iii) even if the value of sensuality is admitted, and even if (i) were not claimed, it could perhaps be argued that still abstention from sensual pleasures is of consequence to one who is without desire or craving for them, no more than food is of use to one already fed.

In addition, it seems the renunciate won't only deny the claim, but assume the opposite stance: namely, that sensuality is life-denying, the path to death proper.
