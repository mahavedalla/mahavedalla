Notes:

First, terms. Sensual pleasure denotes any pleasure mediated by the five physical senses (mental sensual pleasures ignored for convenience for now). Given the connotations of the term "sensual", "sensorial" is perhaps the apter term. In this sense, this includes both carnal sensual pleasures, and the subtle pleasure of viewing a setting sun, or even the pleasure of a well functioning monastic community. An understanding of what sensual craving is will not be addressed here. What abstention is should be addressed here. The aim of the article is not those sensual pleasures about which there is little question they need be abstained from, such as intoxicants, sexuality, etc., but those like the pleasures of nature, spiritual poetry, pleasant food, and so on.

As for Canonical citations, there are the quotes of the Buddha and Arahants about the beauties of nature; there are the allowances in the Vināya for pleasant foods, such as cakes, butter, meats, etc.; MN 26 and the deer who eat the bait without tripping the trap; there are the suttas which describe the monk who eats a delicious meal, and then goes off to meditate; in AN 5.?? there are the benefits of toothwood; there's the Buddha's encouragement of Mahā Kassapa to abandon the austere life; there are discourses where the Buddha speaks of the benefits of giving pleasant offerings the Saṅgha, including pleasant scents.

As for extra Canonical sources, Tan Ajaan Geoff has specifically stated numerous times that not all sensual pleasures are harmful, and thus have to be abstained from.

"Now, because the search for the five cords of sensuality does not just exist for householders, but the ignoble search by way of the five cords of sensuality also exists for homeless ones enjoying the four requisites not having reviewed them, therefore 'Monks, these five cords of sensuality...' is said to show that. Four sensual cords cognizable by the eye which are agreeable, etc., are understood in regard to newly died bowls and robes, etc. However, here the function is usage. Five are understood in regard to delightful alms and medicines. Four as in lodgings and robes. Moreover, here to the function is just usage."

"So he does encourage sensory pleasure in moderation. He says there are pleasures that are perfectly harmless for the mind."

"The pleasures that come from going out into nature are relatively harmless, as are the pleasures of solitude and the pleasures of being in the company of virtuous people when there’s a sense of harmony in the group."

“Remember, the Buddha was afraid of sensual pleasures because they made you heedless.

Of course, not all sensual pleasures do that, but the fascination with planning for sensuality is a heedless pursuit. Sensual pleasures come and go, and as long as they don't have a bad effect on the mind, and the Buddha says that not all sensual pleasures have a bad effect on the mind, then they're okay. It's only when they give rise to a more creative version of delusion, that's when they're a problem.”
