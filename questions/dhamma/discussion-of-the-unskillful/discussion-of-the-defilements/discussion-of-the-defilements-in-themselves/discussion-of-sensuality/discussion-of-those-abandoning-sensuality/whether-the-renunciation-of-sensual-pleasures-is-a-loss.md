# Whether the renunciation of sensual pleasures is a loss?

## Bibliography

<!-- 

Notes:

I interpret this question differently than the question about whether there is any happiness in the absence of desire/craving. This question is about not the pleasure of the gratification of sensual craving, which is negative reinforcement, but about the actual raw, so to speak, pleasant feeling of sensual pleasures. This is separate and would survive the ending of craving, as it is stated by the Buddha that an Arahant continues to feel pleasant feelings; and moreover there are clear instances in the Canon of Arahants expressing their pleasure with sensorial experiences. 

Thus the question is, accepting that the ideal renunciation posited by the Buddha requires abstaining from various sensual pleasures themselves, such as abstaining from sexual intercourse, entertainment, wealth, etc., is this a loss on the part of the renunciate? It seems to me a worthy question, the answer to which will turn on some subtle ideas.

It seems to me now the heart of the response is simply to say no, that the sensual pleasure remaining beyond the gratification of craving is next to grass and twigs. An additional comment is that the predominate pleasure that is derived from indulging in sensual pleasures, is just the relief from craving. Moreover, in the absence of craving for those pleasures the renunciate must abstain from, there would simply be no interest for them at all. No more than I am interested now in watching paint dry or underwater basket weaving, is one free from sensual craving interested in sexuality, romance, entertainment, wealth, cars, etc. Moreover, other pleasures should be mentioned here as well. This question implicitly assumes that beyond sensual pleasures there just isn't much competition, which the Canon resoundingly rejects. By analogy, suppose I decide, due to various issues, to stop purchasing groceries from grocery store A. Then, someone comes along and asks me, "But where will you get your groceries?" Obviously, what is missed in asking the question is that A is just one grocery on the block, and there are others at which I will get my groceries. The fact that I have and do get my groceries from A does not mean I can only get them from A. Similarly, there are other competitors on the block besides the sensual pleasures the renunciate renounces.

The above are just disparate thoughts that need to be developed and woven together into a more coherent answer. The straightforward answer is no: it seems clear to me that the view presented in the Canon is that the raw feeling (vedanā) of sensual pleasures is grass and twigs. There is just not much there.
