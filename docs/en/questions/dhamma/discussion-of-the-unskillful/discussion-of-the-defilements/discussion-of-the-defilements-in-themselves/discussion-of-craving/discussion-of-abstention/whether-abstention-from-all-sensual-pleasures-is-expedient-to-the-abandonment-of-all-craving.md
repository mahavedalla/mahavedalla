# Whether abstention from all sensual pleasures is expedient to the abandonment of all craving?

## Bibliography

<!-- 

Notes:

The scope of this question is as broad as is feasible for a human being: thus all sensual pleasures, perhaps, includes even salt and a sunset and a meal with friends and family. Expediency means optional, not needed strictly or practically, but beneficial. I personally know of no one who explicitly or implicitly adopts this view, and as far as I can see most explicitly or implicitly reject it, such as Taan Ajaan, and even quite austere practitioners like Ajahn Ñāṇamoli. Moreover, I would bet many would declare the inexpediency of this view, perhaps because it is a waste of time, and positvely harmful insofar as it is often motivated by and perpetuating of misunderstandings about the practice. 

The suttas seem to contain an abundance of examples indicating that the view therein is not affirmative, though, as far as I know, neither negative. For instance, I do not recall reading of any stories where a monk had a practice of deliberately choosing to not eat food offered in his bowl which was too decadent. I recall the view being more along the lines of taking whatever is given, fine or coarse, indifferently. Moreover, the suttas variously speak of the beauty and pleasure of nature in a positive light.
