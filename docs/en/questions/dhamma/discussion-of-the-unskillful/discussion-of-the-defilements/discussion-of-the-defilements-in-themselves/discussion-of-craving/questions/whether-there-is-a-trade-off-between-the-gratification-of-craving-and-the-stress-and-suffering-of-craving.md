# Whether there is a trade-off between the gratification of craving and the stress and suffering of craving?

## Bibliography

<!-- 

Notes:

As has already been argued, the gratification of craving itself is merely a negative reinforcement pleasure, akin to recovering from an illness, scratching an intense itch, allaying hunger, and such like; and therefore, it cannot constitute the grounds for a rational trade-off.

Clarification

This question concerns the pleasure involved in the gratification of craving itself, not the raw pleasant qualities of the objects of craving. The issue is whether it is rational to accept the stress and suffering of craving in exchange for the pleasure that accompanies its satisfaction.

Renunciate Response (Concise, with Analogy)

As argued elsewhere, the pleasure associated with the gratification of craving is a relief-pleasure—the alleviation of an affliction—rather than a positive good in itself. Wherefore, its removal is not only not a deprivation, but a gain, just as recovery from illness is not a loss, but a gain.

The raw pleasures of sensuality that remain after craving is removed are, on the Strong R view, next to grass and twigs: they may be present, but they are minor, trivial, and of little motivating or functional significance. One might compare them to the enjoyment of playing with childhood toys as an adult: a faint, harmless pleasure, but not something that contributes meaningfully to one’s well-being.

Furthermore, the renunciate gains access to higher pleasures—such as the delight of freedom from craving, meditative calm, and path-related joys—which are unavailable to one still bound by craving. Taken together, there is no genuine loss to the renunciate.
