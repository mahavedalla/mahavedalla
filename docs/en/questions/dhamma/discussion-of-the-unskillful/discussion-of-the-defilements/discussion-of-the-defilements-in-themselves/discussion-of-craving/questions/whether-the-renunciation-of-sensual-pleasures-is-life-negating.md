# Whether the renunciation of sensual pleasures is life-negating?

## Bibliography

<!-- 

Notes:

Objection (Compressed)

(1) craving appears instrumental to the production of higher goods; (2) much ordinary happiness seems to arise from the gratification of craving; and (3) the renunciation of sensual pleasures appears to involve the loss of genuinely valuable pleasures. Taken together, this suggests that the doctrine is life-negating.

Renunciate Response (Concise)

(1) As argued elsewhere, either what are taken to be “higher goods” are not genuinely so, or craving is not in fact necessary for the production of those higher goods that remain.

(2) As argued elsewhere, the gratification of craving consists in negative reinforcement—the relief of an affliction—and is therefore not a positive good in its own right; indeed, freedom from craving and the need for its relief is itself a great good and pleasure.

(3) As argued elsewhere, the renunciation of sensual pleasures does not constitute a genuine loss to the renunciate. This is because (i) there are pleasures superior to sensual pleasure; (ii) raw sensual pleasure has little value for one who is without craving, just as pleasant food has little value for one who is satiated; and (iii) once craving and its associated perceptual distortion are removed, many sensual pleasures come to appear as they are—unsatisfactory or even unpleasant (cf. MN 75).

Moreover, the renunciation of sensual pleasures conduces to the abandonment of sensual craving, which causes gratuitous stress and suffering to oneself and others, as has been stated elsewhere. Wherefore, not only such renunciation not a loss, but on the contrary the renunciation of sensual pleasures confers life on the renunciate, and is a positive gain, insofar as it frees them from the disease of craving.
