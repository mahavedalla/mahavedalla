# Whether the practice of mettā is beneficial?

## Bibliography

Notes:

AN 3.69:

"‘ko panāvuso, hetu ko paccayo yena anuppanno ceva doso nuppajjati uppanno ca doso pahīyatī’ti? ‘mettā cetovimuttī tissa vacanīyaṃ."

AN 11:15:

(1) sukhaṃ supati

(3) na pāpakaṃ supinaṃ passati
(4) manussānaṃ piyo hoti
(5) amanussānaṃ piyo hoti

Provisionally, benefits could be divided into personal (oneself) and social (others). AN 11.15 provides a fairly robust list of seemingly more personal benefits of developing mettā. Although yet uncited, I recall various suttas where the Buddha describes mettā as a component of communal harmony, a social benefit. Not to be forgotten, one might also maintain that mettā is the primary antidote to aversive emotions, such as anger, cruelty, and such like. It would seem prima facie that the attenuation and elimination of such aversive states is both a personal and social benefit: the former, in brief, because of the freedom from the stress and suffering inherent in said states, and from their unfavorable consequences; the latter, in brief, because of uprooting the causes for the various kinds of verbal and bodily misconduct born of said states, like conflict, violence, war, etc.
