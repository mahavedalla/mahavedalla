# Whether Nibbāna causes the destruction of the defilements?

## Bibliography

<!-- 

Notes:

"dasayime, bhikkhave, dhamme appahāya abhabbo arahattaṃ sacchikātuṃ. katame dasa? rāgaṃ, dosaṃ, mohaṃ..." (AN 10.100)

The preceding might seem to suggest that the defilements have to be abandoned before (in time) Nibbāna can be attained. If abandonment is equated with or mutually entailed by destruction, then this might seem to suggest that the destruction of the defilements must precede the attainment of Nibbāna in time, in which case, on pain of adopting retro-active causation, "taṃ āgamma" is to this extent undermined.
Then again, there might seem to be reason for pause. One might think the sutta does not straightforwardly indicate the temporal priority of thr abandonment of the defilements. Perhaps botht heir abandonment and the realization of Nibbāna occur simultaneously. Or, perhaps the causal relationship is not so rigid as the sutta suggests, in light of the fact that causality all over the suttas involves feedback loops and non-linearity. For instance:

"nandikkhayā rāgakkhayo, rāgakkhayā nandikkhayo" (SN 22.52)

Then again, AN 10.100 doesn't appear to use an ablative to describe the precedential cause, unlike SN 22.52, but the absolutive.

Further, it might seem it is a challenge for timeless unconditionalist views of Nibbāna to explain how a timeless Nibbāna could cause the destruction of the defilements, if "taṃ āgamma" is construed causally, which, perhaps, one might not.
