# Whether in the absence of all desires there is any happiness?

## Bibliography

<!-- 

Notes:

Objection 1: It seems that in the absence of all desires there would be no happiness, for what happiness do we know except that which comes from the gratification or satisfaction of some desire; and thus, devoid of any desire, whence the happiness of the gratification of desire?

Objection 2: Moreover, it seems even the Buddha himself admits that in desirelessness there is no happiness, for it is said: "[Māra] A person's delight comes from acquisitions, since a person with no acquistions doesn't delight....[The Buddha] A person's grief comes from acquisitions, since a person with no acquisitions doesn't grieve." Since the Buddha doesn't reject Māra's assertion, but counters it, he implicitly accepts that in acquisitionlessness, which entails desirelessness, there is no delight; and if there is no delight, whence happiness?

On the contrary, it is written "Happy [susukhaṃ] indeed we live, not zealously desiring, among those zealously desiring" (DHP 199); and in innumerable other ways it has been declared by the Buddha and the noble disciples that not only is desirelessness not devoid of happiness, but, in fact, desirelessness constitutes the highest happiness of living beings.

Reply to Objection 1: It is unobjectionable that the happiness of most beings mostly, if not entirely, consists in the gratification of desire, in particular sensual desire. However, as has been argued (link), the happiness that consists in the gratification of desire (qua passion) is fundamentally beset by stress and suffering, and thus its end, consequent upon the ending of desires, as the end of the aforementioned stress and suffering, brings exclusively happiness. 

Reply to Objection 2: In isolation, it is natural to assume, according to this sutta, that the Buddha concedes to Māra that desirelessness is delightless, and thus happinessless. However, in light of the innumerable statements elsewhere made by the Buddha and the noble disciples which explicitly testify that the attenuation and ending of desire is not only not happinessless, but the highest happiness, the aforesaid position, unqualified, seems untenable. However, if it be qualified thus, as concerning that delight exclusive to the defilements of passion, lust, craving, concupiscence, greed, etc., then desirelessness is very much delightless. Just as the leper cured from their disease no longer can delight in scratching their limbs violently or cauterizing their limbs over an open fire, as they once did, yet is exclusively better for it, just so can the desireless no longer delight in those pleasures inseparable from the aforesaid defilements (e.g., intoxicants, entertainment, sexual intercourse, etc.), yet is exclusively better for it.
