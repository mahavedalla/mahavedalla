# Whether learning is practically necessary?

## Bibliography

<!-- 

Notes:

Practical necessity is still a provisional and ambiguous term, a tentative clarificatory example of which is ordination: perhpaps one might think it possible to become an Arahant without ordination, but nonetheless practically necessary in almost all cases. Necessity here is measured in relation to Arahantship, for now. Thus, the practical necessity of learning with respect to teaching or such like is not addressed here. Though my investigations there aren't complete, it seems doubtless that learning is expedient: that is, necessity aside, it is very useful along the path to be learned in the canon (and perhaps other material besides). The question of the modality of time has been mentioned in a similar question regarding beneficiality. One might think that it was once not practically necessary, but now it is.
Again, further disruptive research notwithstanding, it seems clear that the suttas indicate much learning was not practically necessary, but left and right people were attaining after only a single discourse. The question of the independence of the stream-enterer is relevant here. Should the bar for this quedtion be mere stream-entry, the thought being that the stream-enterer, though they might benefit from learning more, does not strictly need for the final goal any further instruction? With thisnin mind, it would seem practical necessity does not obtain for the Buddha's time. I am less confident about our time. Then again, last I checked there is no public data on who in our age is attained and not, and so it is difficult to say with hugh confidence that we now need more instruction, though that is my leaning. It is unclesr whatbthe reasoning for this position would be.
