# Whether the abandonment of craving by the renunciation of most sensual pleasures is a loss to the renunciate?

## Bibliography

<!-- 

Notes:

Clarifying the Question

The objection assumes that by renouncing most sensual pleasures, the renunciate not only abandons craving but also the raw enjoyment associated with these pleasures. If this raw enjoyment is valuable, then giving it up might seem to constitute a genuine loss, raising the question of whether the path entails sacrificing meaningful pleasure for the sake of liberation.

Renunciate Response (Concise)

As argued elsewhere, the pleasure associated with the gratification of craving is a relief-pleasure (the alleviation of an affliction) rather than a positive good in itself. Its removal is therefore not a deprivation, any more than recovery from illness or scratching an itch is a loss.

The raw pleasure of sensuality that remains after craving is removed is, by analogy, like food for someone who is already full: it may be present, but it is of no use to one who is free from craving. Furthermore, once craving and its perceptual distortions are removed, many formerly enjoyable experiences appear neutral or even unpleasant (cf. MN 75).

Finally, the renunciate also gains access to higher pleasures—such as the delight of freedom from craving, meditative calm, and other path-related enjoyments—which are unavailable to one still bound by craving. Taken together, there is no genuine loss to the renunciate. The loss is in fact for the ordinary individual.
