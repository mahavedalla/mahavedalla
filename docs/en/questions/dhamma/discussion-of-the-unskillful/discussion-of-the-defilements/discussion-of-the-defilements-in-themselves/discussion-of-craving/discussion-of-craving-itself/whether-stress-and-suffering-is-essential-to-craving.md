# Whether stress and suffering is essential to craving?

## Bibliography

<!-- 

Notes:

In other words, is it possible, perhaps not in the actual world (metaphysically), that there be craving and no stress and suffering. One might be inclined to say that stress and suffering are essential to craving. One reason is that this might seem to provide a clear conduit to direct knowledge that craving is universally (as far as the four noble truths are concerned) and invariably the cause of suffering: stress and suffering are just essential to craving. For instance, just as through acquaintance with water through scientific investigation we might know that whatever water there is, here, yonder, then, etc., all that must be composed of H2O. One concern is whether this yields a vacuous victory for the insight of the four noble truths; in other words, did the four noble truths simply get defined into truth? Perhaps this intuition can be challenged again with reference to the analogy of water (assuming the metaphysical necessity of the chemical constitution for the sake of argument): one might think water is essentially H2O, yet this neither seems like a vacuous truth, but a genuine insight resulting from extensive investigation into empirical reality.
