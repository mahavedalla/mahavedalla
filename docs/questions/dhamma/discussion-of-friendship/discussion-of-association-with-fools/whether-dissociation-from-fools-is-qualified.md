# Whether dissociation from fools is unqualified?

## Bibliography

<!-- 

Notes:

In other words, does dissociation simply mean that one should not in any capacity, physical or otherwise, interact with another person, or rather that they shouldn't in certain capacities, such as, say, in teaching?

"evarūpo, bhikkhave, puggalo na sevitabbo na bhajitabbo na payirupāsitabbo aññatra anuddayā aññatra anukampā." (AN 3.26)

The above excerpt would seem to provide what to many would seem to be the obvious answer: that dissasociation from fools is qualified, so that it doesn't simply mean cutting all ties outright with fools without exception. One could read the excerpt suggests that it is at least permissible to associate with fools out of compassion for them. 
Nonetheless, one might read the discourse differently as applying only people who are actually on the path or developing it, who cannot be considered fools outright. Thus, on this view, the discourse says, in affect, regarding the path, seek equals or superiors, and associste with inferiors (not mecessarily fools) only from compassion for them.
