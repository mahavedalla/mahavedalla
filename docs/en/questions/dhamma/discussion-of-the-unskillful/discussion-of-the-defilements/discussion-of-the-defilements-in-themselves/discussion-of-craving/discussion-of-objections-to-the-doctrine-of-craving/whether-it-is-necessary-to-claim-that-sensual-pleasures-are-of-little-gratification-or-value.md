# Whether it is necessary to claim that sensual pleasures are of little gratification or value?

## Bibliography

<!-- 

Notes:

Response

While a faithful defense of early Buddhist renunciation will assert that raw sensual pleasures are of little gratification or value, this claim is not strictly necessary for the dialectic. What is essential is the claim that whatever goods or pleasures there are in sensual pleasures are outweighed by those connected with renunciation. And the manner in which this is established is twofold:

(1) Independently of their value, raw sensual pleasures are of little interest to one free from craving, much like food, even sumptuous and refined, is of little value to someone who is satiated.

(2) By appeal to superior pleasures, the renunciate has access to pleasures of the path and freedom from craving, which are at least comparable to any remaining sensual pleasures.

Thus, the argument does not require the strong claim that sensual pleasures are negligible in themselves; it only requires that renunciation does not deprive the renunciate of genuine satisfaction or comparable forms of pleasure that can pull weight, as it were, on the scale against sensual pleasure.
