# Whether love is a defilement?

## Bibliography

<!-- 

Notes:

It currently seems to me that "pema" like "chandas" is a multi-faceted term, which cannot be called unwholesome without qualification, or something like that. This is perhaps not unlike the English term "love", one translation of "pema" I have heard. The following is a mere cursory attempt to describe, on my own terms, two kinds of love. In English, it seems love can refer both to an instinctive and involuntary emotion, often found between family and friends; this love might often be primarily motivated by the pleasure offer of the object or its relation to the subject, and might appear partial, and lead to other such emotions as sorrow, anxiety, anger, and so on, according to the different ways the object or the subject's relation thereto is altered (e.g., losing a beloved family member causes sorrow). Love might also refer to an acquired and proactive emotion, as in religious contexts, or in some social movements, motivated more by something intrinsic to the object, less its benefit to the subject, such as innate dignity or moral worth. 
In some passages, it seems "pema" (especially in regard to a teacher) is spoken of positively. In others, it seemes to be placed on a par with "chandas" and "rāga" (SN 22.87), and is described as an emotion which is intimately linked to "dosa" (hatred; like love and hate), abandoned in the meditative attainments, and made non-existent and no longer subject to future arising ("anabhāvaṃkataṃ āyatiṃ anuppādadhammaṃ") in the case of the Arahant (AN 4.200).
