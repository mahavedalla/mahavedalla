# Whether occurrent pleasures contradict that craving causes suffering?

## Bibliography

<!-- 

Notes:

Borrowed from phil/scientific literature. "occurrent pleasure" here is one that arises seemingly without prior craving. E.g., I experience something sensually pleasurable that I had never experienced before nor knew about. 

If part of the claim is that most of the pleasure of sensuality is a negative reinforcement pleasure (a pleasure of relief from craving), then it might seem like occurrent pleasures challenge this, since, if they exist, there was no prior craving for the object. Moreover, it seems plausible that some occurrent pleasures are clearly sensual, not mere pleasant feeling.

One obvious response is that, per dependent origination, craving can be conditioned by feeling, and thus the occurrent pleasure simultaneously gives rise to craving.

It is interesting to consider how craving could arise simultaneously with an occurrent pleasure, and to explain the negative reinforcement arising simultaneously, which explains the sensual aspect of the occurrent pleasure, assuming there is one. An itch analogy could potentially explain it.
