# Whether self-love is a defilement?

## Bibliography

<!-- 

Notes:

There is a question about how self-love should be understood in the Canon. If "love" is read as "pema", then the prospects for an undefiled self-love seem fewer. If "love" is divested of its affectionate connotations and is understood merely along the lines of a wish for one's own true happiness and pursuing that, then the prospects seem good.

Famous exchange between King Pasenadi and Mallika. Also SN 1.13.

"sabbā disā anuparigamma cetasā, nevajjhagā piyataramattanā kvaci" (SN 3.8)

"natthi attasamaṃ pemaṃ" (SN 1.13)

As much as one might be inclined to regard these statements as tacit endorsements of self-love, one might think the statements are merely descriptive, not evaluative. 

"natthi puttasamaṃ pemaṃ" (SN 1.13)

"natthi rāgasamo aggi, natthi dosasamo kali,
 natthi khandhasamā dukkhā"

The latter can hardly be taken as an endorsement, yet have the same form as the former. Moreover, AN 4.200 might seem to suggest that there is no pema for an Arahant,

"yasmiṃ, bhikkhave, samaye bhikkhu āsavānaṃ khayā anāsavaṃ cetovimuttiṃ paññāvimuttiṃ diṭṭheva dhamme sayaṃ abhiññā sacchikatvā upasampajja viharati, yampissa pemā pemaṃ jāyati tampissa pahīnaṃ hoti ucchinnamūlaṃ tālāvatthukataṃ anabhāvaṃkataṃ āyatiṃ anuppādadhammaṃ"
