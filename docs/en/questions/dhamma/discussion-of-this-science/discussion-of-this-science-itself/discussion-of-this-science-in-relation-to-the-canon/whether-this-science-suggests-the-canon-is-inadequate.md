# Whether this science suggests the Canon is inadequate?

## Bibliography

<!-- 

Notes:

Objection 1: It seems that this science suggests the Canon is inadequate, for if the Canon were adequate, no further science thereof would be necessary. Yet, it was argued in (...citation...) that this science is necessary; wherefore, this science implies the inadequacy of the Canon (by modus tollens).

The Buddha said that his teachings can be divided in two ways: (1) teachings the meaning of which shouldn't be interpreted and (2) teachings the meaning of which should be interpreted. Prima facie, the simple subjunctive conditional, "If the Canon were adequate, no further science would be necessary" may appear plausible. However, there is reason to doubt it on further inspection. Why? One, the Canon does not nor could be expected to address every possible question about the Dhamma. Two, one might think it is not the adequacy of the Canon which is in question, but rather the changing times; in other words, as times change, new and unforeseen questions arise in relation to the Dhamma and Vinaya, and thus it might seem necessary that there be some science which can interpret the Canon in light of the new and unique needs of a particular time. And so the practitioner has to craft a Dhammic response to mono-theistic objection at one time, or clarify the scope of certain precepts with changes in technology, and so on. Then again, this could be objected to by citing the various occasions where the Dhamma is described as "timeless" in the sense of being always suitable and relevant, regardless of time.

Nonetheless, this could all be objected to as more or less affirming the objection, as it seems both reasons suggest the Canon is not itself capable of responding to the changing times, even if it is reasonable to think that the Canon shouldn't be expected to (since it was formed thousands of years ago largely within the confines of the thought world of India and other east Asian regions).

The question perhaps should also be clarified, as "inadequate" is somewhat ambiguous. Another approach would be that it is not the adequacy of the Canon which is in question, but rather since people are wont to misinterpret the Canon, it is necessary that there be a science to clarify, defend, and contest misunderstandings. It could also simply be conceded that while the content of the Canon is adequate, there is a need to better organize said content to address specific questions, which is one function of this science. The issue is this doesn't seem to be the only or even main function of this science.
