# Whether impersonal things themselves, such as skillful qualities or liberation, can be paid homage to?

AN 7.32: "satthugaru dhammagaru, saṅghe ca tibbagāravo, samādhigaru ātāpī". "One with respect for the teacher and Dhamma, and strong respect for the Saṅgha, one who is ardent with respect for concentration."

The Mora Paritta: "Namo bodhiyā...namo vimuttiyā". Is there not also the case of the Buddha paying homage to the Dhamma after the awakening?

There is also the case of the institution of the Dhamma and Vinaya becoming the teacher after the Buddha's Parinibbāna. There is also the Buddha's famous words on what the proper way to reverence him is.

1. Anti-realism
2. Realism

The anti-realist family encompasses views that deny that impersonal things, such as qualities, can be the objects of homage. The realist position affirms as much. The analogical interpretation would seem to be anti-realist, and the objectual and personal interpretations realist. The anti-realist's burden is to provide a plausible explanation of Canonical evidence and practical experience which seems to suggest the affirmative. The realist's burden is to explain just what the object is supposed to be. The analogical interpretation attempts to fulfill the burden by claiming that usages of reverential terminology with respect to impersonal objects can be understood in a different sense of reverence, devotion, and so on.

Q: Whether inanimate qualities can be taken as an object of veneration?

A: Yes.

I asked specifically with reference to the Mora Paritta, quoting “Nam’atthu bodhiyā…namo vimuttiyā”. Tan Ajaan responded affirming the question and himself citing that the Buddha venerated (or some similar verb) the Dhamma.

I then questioned about what precisely the object is in the case of awakening and liberation. His response seemed to be that the object is one’s own potential to awakening and liberation and that other’s have awakened and liberated themselves, and that one has respect for that. The latter, as stated, is somewhat unclear, due to my poor recall. Is the object there the person, or their actual awakening/liberations, or their potential? 

I also asked explicitly, somewhat rhetorically, if there was an awakening substrate that is the object of veneration, which he denied. He the clarified again that it is your idea of awakening/liberation that is (or is also) the object. I should have asked what the ontological status of that idea is. Is it an abstract object? A type? A useful convention?

It is unclear where to classify Tan Ajaan's view. He did explicitly say that an impersonal object can be the object of veneration, which would seem to be realist according to the above classification. Nonetheless, the sense in which he used the term "veneration" is not entirely clear. I am for now inclined to take it in the reverential sense, thus realist.

One issue that could arise for the view is this: if one oneself can venerate their potential to awakening, liberation, etc., why couldn't another? And, could this lead to a kind of venerational explosion wherein everyone is worthy of veneration?
