# Whether no-self continuity interpretations of the tetralemma affirm the fourth member?

## Bibliography

<!-- 

Notes:

This question was inspired by a Q&A with Taan Ajaan. I am not qualified to represent faithfully the details of Taan Ajaan's argument, so the following should be read as an argument independent of Taan Ajaan to avoid misrepresenation:

Some interpret the tetralemma as referring to the continuation of the Tathāgata at death (i.e., continues to exist, ceases to exist...), and they maintain that since the Tathāgata has no self, he cannot continue to exists, cease to, etc., and thus the tetralemma doesn't apply.
However, on this interpretation of the tetralemma and anattā, the fourth member would apply: that is, the Tathāgata neither continues to exist nor ceases to exist.

I am as yet undecided about this argument. However, I will pose a hypothetical rejoinder. Firstly, how precisely should the tetralemma, on the continuity view proposed, be logically analyzed? Here is one way.

M1: the Tathāgata continues to exist
M2: the Tathāgata cease to exist
M3: the Tathāgata both continues to and ceases to exist
M4: the Tathāgata neither continues to nor ceases to exist.

The logical structure of M1-3 all seem clear. What about M4?

Option 1: ~M1&~M2 ≈ ~(M1|M2) (DeMorgan)
Option 2: ~M1|~M2 ≈ ~(M1&M2) (DeMorgan)

Prima facie, option 1 seems the better interpretation to me.

Now, onto the rejoinder. It seems Taan Ajaan's objection would succeed if the argument implied that M1 is false and M2 is false, as (~M1&~M2), which is M4, would follow by any standard logic, as far as I am aware.
However, why could someone with a no-self continuity interpretation not maintain that M1 and M2 do not apply, or have no truth value, and that thus M3 and M4 cannot either? For instance, if the original arguer maintains that M1 and M2 have no truth-value because the name Tathāgata fails to refer (perhaps as Strawsonian view), then it seems they can also maintain that M3 and M4 likewise do not apply.

An objection to this rejoinder could be that it assumes a non-standard classical-logical view that permits truth-value gaps.

A further rejoinder to this might be that while this might be a general objection to the rejoinder, it is toothless in this dialectic, since any interpretation of the tetralemma will have to permit truth-value gaps, assuming that the tetralemma must not apply, in the sense of none of its members having a truth-value; e.g., Taan Ajaan and a no-self continuity theorist alike must assume that M1 is neither true nor false.

The latter rejoinder could perhaps be objected to by introducing distinctions in the meaning of applicability in the context of the tetralemma. I leave this here for now.

If this rejoinder succeeds, it would seem to show one can at least defend a no-self continuity interpretation against such an objection. It doesot, however, seem to show that interpretation is correct.

"na upapajjatīti kho, vaccha, na upeti". "tena hi, bho gotama, upapajjati ca na ca upapajjatī"ti? "upapajjati ca na ca upapajjatīti kho, vaccha, na upeti". keci "evaṃ vimuttacitto pana, bho gotama, bhikkhu kuhiṃ 'upapajjatī'tiādi: so pubbe maraṇā vijjantaṃ tathāgatassa attaṃ maññitvā pucchati. yassa yena upapajjanacoditā, so eva paramatthato nupalabbhati yathā vañjhātanayassa matteyyatākittanaṃ. tena hi, "upapajjatī"tiādi na upetī"ti.

keci "taṃ na sundaraṃ. kasmā? catutthakoṭiyā upetatāpajjanato. tathāgato ce nupalabbhati, kiṃ so upapajjissati vā na vā upapajjissati. tasmā paṭhamadutiyāya koṭiyā micchattā. atha "neva upapajjati na na upapajjatī"ti āpajjanato, catutthakoṭi upeti. na pana upeti, yathāha:

'neva upapajjati na na upapajjatīti kho, vaccha, na upetī'ti."

nevāhaṃ idaṃ abhinandāmi na paṭikkosāmi. ayaṃ pana me vicāraṇā.

tattha siyā "tathāgatassa anupalabbhanato paṭhamadutiyāya anupetatā, na micchatā. tena hi catutthāya anupetatā, na saccatā. evaṃ catukoṭi rakkhitā"ti.
