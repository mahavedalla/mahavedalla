# Whether it is necessary to claim that stress and suffering are essential to craving?

## Bibliography

<!-- 

Notes:

It doesn't seem necessary, though that seems to be the claim found in the Canon, and not an implausible one. It is merely necessary to claim that where there is craving, there is almost always gratuitous stress and suffering, the appropriate response to which is renunciation. For instance, the flu is something we all largely agree should be avoided, eliminated, cured, etc.; however, the flu doesn't always negatively affect someone who contracts it; it is just generally detrimental to human health and well-being. Likewise, strong renunciation can be defended without claiming that craving in every case harms one affected by it. 

To claim stress and suffering are essential to craving, while, if true, would make craving that much more problematic, it also weakens the argument, because the claim is that much stronger.
