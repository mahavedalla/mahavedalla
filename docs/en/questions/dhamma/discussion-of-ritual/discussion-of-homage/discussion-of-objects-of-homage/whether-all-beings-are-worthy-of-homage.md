# Whether all beings are worthy of homage?

## Bibliography

<!-- 

Notes:

"evamevaṃ kho, bhikkhave, pañcahi aṅgehi samannāgato bhikkhu na āhuneyyo hoti na pāhuneyyo na dakkhiṇeyyo na añjalikaraṇīyo na anuttaraṃ puññakkhettaṃ lokassa. katamehi pañcahi? idha, bhikkhave, bhikkhu akkhamo hoti rūpānaṃ, akkhamo saddānaṃ, akkhamo gandhānaṃ, akkhamo rasānaṃ, akkhamo phoṭṭhabbānaṃ." (AN 5.139)

One might be inclined to think that the question is not to be answered categorically, but analytically, following the above quotation, taking "añjali-karaṇīyo" to be more or less equivalent to "worthy of homage": namely, something like, whatever beings are endowed with virtue, concentration, and discernment, are worthy of homage, and whatever are not, etc.

The question does call for clarity about what homage is: is it mere physical gestures of respect, like material offerings and prostration, or is it a mental or cardiological attitude?
