# Whether stress and suffering are intrinsic or extrinsic to craving?

## Bibliography

<!-- 

Notes:

Craving seems to be identified as the principal cause of suffering in the four noble truths (putting aside the reasonable interpretation that the four noble truths are a summative guide, using craving heuristically as the principal cause, while there are still other principal causes, like delusion and aversion). Yet, one might have the intuition that craving is itself also intrinsically stressful. In this sense, stress and suffering could be essential to craving insofar as they are essential effects thereof, but craving could itself not be suffering or stressful. The question is: yet, is craving in fact alone, effects aside, stressful? I recall discourses which seem to almost conflate upādāna with chandarāga, suggesting the distinction between upādāna (the stated identity of suffering as far as the four noble truths are concerned) and its cause in taṇhā is not so clear-cut.
