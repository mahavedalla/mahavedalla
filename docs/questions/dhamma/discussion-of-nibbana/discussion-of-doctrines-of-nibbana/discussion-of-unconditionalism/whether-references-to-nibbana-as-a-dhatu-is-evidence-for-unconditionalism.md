# Whether references to Nibbāna as a 'dhātu' is evidence for unconditionalism?

Notes:

Bhikkhu Bodhi: "...so I say that there are these terms with an ontological significance that the Buddha uses. Some of the words are 'dhātu', which we often translate as 'element', 'āyatana' ('base'), 'pada' or 'state', and even the word 'dhamma', which has a very broad meaning.

Doug: "The sutta, as we've seen, that describes Nirvāṇa as...uh....an element, actually says that there are two Nirvāṇa elements, as you've just seen: one with something left over, and one with nothing left over. Does that mean we're supposed to assume that there are two different kinds of Nirvāṇa? I don't know of anybody who accepts a view like that."

Potential Rejoinder:

For instance, the morning star is just Venus at sunrise, and the evening star is just Venus at sunset. Both references have the same referent—namely, Venus—but they are distinguished by the different positions of Venus relative to the earth, or something like that. Similarly, perhaps the rejoinder would go, the sa-upādisesā Nibbāna-dhātu is just the one Nibbāna-dhātu, but while the aggregates are still intact; and the an-upādisesā Nibbāna-dhātu is the very same Nibbāna-dhātu, but with aggregates once and for all ceased. In other words, the two uses of 'dhātu' in UD (8.1?) are distinguished not by the referent, but by extrinsic relations therewith, or something like that. 

Further:

Other rejoinders might succeed only to concede that 'dhātu' can be used in a non-ontologically committal fashion, in which case this leaves the door open for the opponent to maintain that this is so in UD (8.1), thus undermining the original position that the use of 'dhātu' is strong evidence of the ontology reality of Nibbāna; however, the aforesaid rejoinder seems to avoid this: both uses of Nibbāna have ontological import, but one and the same referent.
