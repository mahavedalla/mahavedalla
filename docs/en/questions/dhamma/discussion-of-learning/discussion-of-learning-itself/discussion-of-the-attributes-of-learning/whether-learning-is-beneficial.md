# Whether learning is beneficial?

<!--



-->

Presently, the intention of this question is to make progress towards the right view of intellectual study along the path. It is as yet unclear precisely what citations should be sought for, and what would constitute a decisive answer to this question. Generally, it seems the suttas often speak positively about individuals who are learned in the one or another of the bodies of texts (Dhamma or Vinaya), and praise learning itself. There are, nonetheless, other suttas which caution the potential pitfalls of learning.
The question was originally formulated without sense of a clear distinction between levels of relevance to practice: necessary (the difference between this and the following is not entirely clear; here an example of a necessary practice would be virtue, and perhaps an example of the following would be ordaining; I understand there are cases of lay Arahants, but, practically, one needs monastic life to reach the final goal), practically necessary, and expedient. The question is supposed to be relevant to the long-standing charge of practitioners who discount learning, typically emphasizing a practice seen as mostly not including intellectual study. There may also be those who take the opposite stance. Not addressed for now.
Thus, does the question concern expediency or practical necessity? In any case, perhaps both should be answered in some place or other. Another relevant factor is time and place. One might think that while perhaps much study was not practically necessary for the ripe disciples of the Buddha's time and world, we today, separated a long way, are not so ripe and thus might practically need much more study.

Praising learning or the learned:

"bhikkhu, bhikkhave, viyatto vinīto visārado bahussuto dhammadharo dhammānudhammappaṭipanno saṅghaṃ sobheti" (AN 4.7; repeated for nuns, laymen, and laywomen)

"bahussutopi ce hoti, sīlesu susamāhito, ubhayena naṃ pasaṃsanti, sīlato ca sutena ca." (AN 4.6)

"bahussuto hoti sutadharo sutasannicayo" (AN 10.33)

Much learning:

"catuppadāya cepi, bhikkhu, gāthāya atthamaññāya dhammamaññāya dhammānudhammappaṭipanno hoti bahussuto dhammadharoti alaṃ vacanāyā"ti." (AN 4.186)

"dandho, bhikkhave, satuppādo, atha so satto khippaṃyeva visesagāmī hoti" (AN 4.191)

"katamañca, bhikkhave, sutadhanaṃ? idha[P 4.6], bhikkhave, ariyasāvako bahussuto hoti sutadharo sutasannicayo" (AN 7.6)
