# What are the reasons for fasting?

## Introduction

There are four families of reasons for fasting:
1. Spiritual
2. Physical
3. Practical
4. Conventional

Let us examine each family in turn.

## 1. Ascetic reasons for fasting

The first family of reasons for family is that, under the proper direction and within the limits of good health, it can be used to develop skillful qualities—

1. Fewness of wishes
2. Contentment
3. Self-effacement
4. Being easy to support
5. Arousal of energy

—and to abandon unskillful qualities—

1. Sensual craving
2. Discontentment
3. Self-absorption
4. Demandingness
5. Sluggishness

For it is said:

>kiñcāpi so, bhikkhave, bhikkhu taṃ piṇḍapātaṃ bhuñjitvā jighacchādubbalyaṃ paṭivinodetvā evaṃ taṃ rattindivaṃ vītināmeyya, atha kho asuyeva me purimo bhikkhu pujjataro ca pāsaṃsataro ca. taṃ kissa hetu? tañhi tassa, bhikkhave, bhikkhuno appicchatāya santuṭṭhiyā sallekhāya subharatāya vīriyārambhāya saṃvattissati

Monks, even though that monk, having eaten that alms, having dispelled hunger and fatigue, thus would pass the day and night, that earlier monk [who refused the alms and passed the day and night hungry and weak], however, is more honorable and more praiseworthy to me. For what reason? Because, monks, that will lead to that monk's fewness of wishes, contentment, self-effacement, being easy to support, and arousal of energy. (MN 3)

## 2. Physical reasons for fasting

The second family of reasons for fasting concern physical health. Albeit less noble, fasting can improve the health and vitality of one fit to undertake it. The Buddha himself describes three different physical reasons for undertaking a regular practice of fasting: 
1. Improved immunity to illness
2. Greater lightness of body 
3. Increased energy

## 3. Practical

The third reason for fasting is practical. The consumption of food generally costs time and energy in planning, preparing, eating, and cleaning up after the meal. By fasting, time can be divested from these activities to more productive ones, such as meditation, study, work, service to others, etc.

## 4. Conventional

The fourth reason for fasting is conventional. This particular reason does not so much apply generally, but to samaṇas in particular. In ancient India during the life of the Buddha, it was customary and expected that samaṇas would, in virtue of their status as samaṇas, refrain from eating at the wrong time of roughly after midday until the following dawn, and thus fast. Respect for this ancient convention is thus another reason for samaṇas in particular, though all those who appreciate it, to fast regularly.
