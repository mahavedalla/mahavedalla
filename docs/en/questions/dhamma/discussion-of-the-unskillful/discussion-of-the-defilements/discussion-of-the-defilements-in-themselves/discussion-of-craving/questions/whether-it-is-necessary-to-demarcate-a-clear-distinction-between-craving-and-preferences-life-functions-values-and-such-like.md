# Whether it is necessary to demarcate a clear distinction between craving and preferences, life functions, values, and such like?

## Bibliography

<!-- 

Notes:

It seems it is certainly good to clearly distinguish craving/desire qua defilement and craving/desire qua non-defilement. I also hope to do so. Nonetheless, it doesn't seem necessary. I know blue is distinct from yellow, but I can't explain electromagnetic radiation and the relevant physics. The distinction between craving/desire qua defilement and that qua non-defilement, while at times, although the line between them is somewhat blurry, are clearly distinct to me and many others as well, without need to provide an analytical account of that distinction. This seems to be not only true in Buddhism, but all other major religions which recognize a distinction between lust, greed, gluttony, passion, etc., and ordinary and even healthy forms of wanting, zeal, desire, and so on. (Such religions of course do not agree on what wantings fall under which; e.g., it seems any kind of sexual activity is going to involve lust according to the suttas, while this is not so in the Abrahamic religions.)
