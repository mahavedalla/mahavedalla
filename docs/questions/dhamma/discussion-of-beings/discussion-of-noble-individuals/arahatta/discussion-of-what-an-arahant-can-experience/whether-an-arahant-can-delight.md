# Whether an Arahant can delight?

## Objections

### Objection 1

It seems an Arahant cannot delight, for it is said: 

>From delight is born grief,\
from delight is born fear.\
For one freed from delight\
there's no grief\
— so how fear? (DHP 214, Ṭhānissaro)

However, since there is neither fear, nor grief in an Arahant, neither is there delight; therefore, an Arahant cannot delight.

## Replies

On the contrary, it is said, "Since I take delight in that, I do not envy what is inferior, nor do I delight therein." (MN 75, Bodhi)

### Reply to Objection 1

A distinction must be drawn between delight as such, or unqualified, and delight qua defilement, or delight born of defilement. The latter, while a species of the former, does not exhaust it; and so there are delights apart from the defilements. Therefore, indeed, an Arahant freed from the defilements is incapable of experiencing any delight qua defilement; however, not only *can* an Arahant experience those delights apart from defilement, such as the delight of concentration, or of liberation, but they *live* in delight, as it is said:

>In village or wilds,\
valley, plateau:\
that place is delightful\
where arahants dwell. (DHP 98, Ṭhānissaro)

Thus, the aforementioned quote, when referring to "delight", is to be understood as referring to delight qua defilement, not delight as such.
