# Whether immoral conduct is a drawback of sensual craving?

## Objections

### Objection 1

It seems immoral conduct is not a drawback of sensual craving, for the gratification of sensual craving induces pleasure in the gratifier. Now, pleasure conduces to clarity of mind, and clarity of mind conduces to discernment of what is moral and immoral, and discernment of what is moral and immoral conduces to moral conduct. Therefore, immoral conduct is not a drawback of sensual craving.

### Objection 2

Moreover, love is inseparable from sensual craving, for what is loved is craved, and what is craved is loved. Now, sensible people agree that love—of living beings for living beings—is a wholesome state that conduces to many wholesome states. In particular, love conduces to giving, sacrifice, selflessness, truthfulness, fidelity, and so on; and such wholesome states conduce to moral conduct, not to immoral conduct. Therefore, owing to sensual craving being inseparable from love, and the latter conducing to various wholesome states, which themselves conduce to moral conduct, immoral conduct is not a drawback of sensual craving.

## Replies

On the contrary, it is said, "[quote from MN 13 or 14 or AN 3.65-66]". 

There is a need to highlight precisely what about sensual craving conduces to immoral conduct, and, if possible, how. What about it: intoxication->heedless->impaired judgement; craving is inherently a state which is at least willing to disregard the interests of others and to harm others in pursuit of its object; it is craving for craving's sake.

Objection 2: Ajaan Geoff concedes that love, in the ordinary sense, such as the love of family, or of wife, does involve or conduce to wholesome states like those mentioned. I don't necessarily want to reject as much. However, ordinary love can nonetheless conduce to wholesome states and still also conduce to immoral conduct. Here I would probably just make mention to another article in which the question of immoral conduct and love is considered itself.
