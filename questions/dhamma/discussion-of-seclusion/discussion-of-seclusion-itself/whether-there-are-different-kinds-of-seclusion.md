# Whether there are different kinds of seclusion?

## Bibliography

<!-- 

Notes:

I recall the Canon distinguishing between three. Kāyaviveka (physical seclusion) could be further divided as into the following categories, not necessarily mutually exclusive:

1. Spatial proximity
2. Perception
3. Interaction
4. Participation
5. Accessibility

Below are very tentative definitions:

1. Spatial isolation: The physical separation of a person from other human bodies, measured by distance, intervening space, and material boundaries.

2. Perception: Sensory awareness of human presence or human representation. For bodily seclusion, the primary form is direct perception of animate humans who are physically present; mediated live and representational perception may be recorded separately.

3. Interaction: Reciprocal exchange with another person through speech, gesture, bodily movement, touch, or other communicative or responsive activity. It may occur directly or through mediation.

4. Participation: Involvement in a shared communal practice, institution, ritual, or pattern of life. Participation may be substantial even when direct interaction is limited, and it may occur through mediated or periodic involvement.

5. Accessibility: The practical ease or difficulty with which another person can reach the secluded person’s location. It includes distance, terrain, infrastructure, transport, navigability, and the physical effort required. Normative permission to enter is a separate matter.

These are not intended to be mutually exclusive and exhaustive.
